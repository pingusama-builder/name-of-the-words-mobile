# Name of the Words: Project Overview

## Part 1: For Everyone — Understanding the App

### Philosophy

**To name is to evoke.**

Name of the Words is a thoughtfully designed platform for collecting, rating, and exploring words that resonate across languages, contexts, and meanings. It recognizes that words are not mere labels—they are vessels of understanding, carriers of cultural weight, and bridges between concepts. Each word becomes a portal to deeper comprehension of language, thought, and self.

---

## What Is Name of the Words?

Imagine a personal library, but instead of books, you collect words. Not random words, but words that move you—words that capture something ineffable, words that are beautiful, words that reveal new layers of meaning each time you encounter them.

Name of the Words is that library. It's a space for slow, deliberate engagement with language. A place where you can:

- **Collect words** that resonate with you, from any language or context
- **Rate them across three dimensions** (Essence, Beauty, Subtlety) to understand what makes each word special
- **Pair words across languages** to explore how different cultures conceptualize the same reality
- **Organize by source** to revisit words from your favorite books, conversations, or articles
- **Switch between modes** depending on whether you're collecting casually or studying deeply
- **Share collections** with others to spark conversation about language and meaning
- **Explore your collection** through multiple lenses: calendar view, tag cloud, source browsing

## Core Features

### 1. Multidimensional Rating System

Words are complex. A word can be profound but plain. Beautiful but shallow. Subtle but less essential. Rather than a single star rating, Name of the Words rates each word across three dimensions:

| Dimension | What It Measures | Example |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| **Essence** (0–12) | How fundamentally important or true does this word feel? Does it capture something real about the world? | "Entropy" rates high on Essence for physicists; "serendipity" rates high on Beauty but lower on Essence |
| **Beauty** (0–12) | How aesthetically or emotionally resonant is this word? Does it please you when spoken or read? | "Mellifluous" is beautiful to say; "water" is essential but less beautiful |
| **Subtlety** (0–12) | How much depth does this word contain? How many layers of meaning does it reveal over time? | "Nostalgia" reveals new layers with each encounter; "happy" is straightforward |

Each word receives a **Match Percentage** calculated from these three dimensions. A perfect match (100%) represents a word that is essential, beautiful, and subtle all at once—rare and precious.

### 2. Paired Meanings Across Languages

Language is not translation—it is transformation. A Chinese word cannot be "translated" into English; it can only be paired with an English word that captures a related but distinct concept.

Name of the Words allows you to collect a word in any language and pair it with a word from another language that resonates with similar meaning. You can rate how well they align and add context explaining the connection. This feature reveals the gaps and bridges between languages. It shows how different cultures conceptualize the same reality differently. It becomes a tool for linguistic and cultural exploration.

### 3. Dual Collection Modes

**Normal Mode:** Collect freely. Add words as you encounter them. Build your personal lexicon without constraints. This mode is for discovery, for following your curiosity, for building a collection that reflects your interests and encounters.

**Work Mode:** Focus deeply on a curated subset. Use this mode when you're researching a specific topic, preparing for a project, or diving deep into a particular domain. Work mode words are visually distinct and can be toggled on/off to filter your view.

**Mutual-Arising:** Some words belong to both Normal and Work collections. They are words you encounter casually but also study deeply. Words that bridge your casual interests and focused work. Mark any word as "Mutual-Arising" to make it visible in all modes simultaneously.

### 4. Organize by Source

Every word has a story. Where did you find it? In which book? On which page? From which conversation?

Name of the Words lets you tag each word with its source (book title, article, show, podcast) and location (page number, chapter, timestamp). The app then groups your words by source, so you can:

- Revisit all words from your favorite book
- Explore how an author uses language
- See which sources have enriched your collection the most
- Swipe through words from a single source in a dedicated card view

### 5. Calendar Visualization

See your collection unfold over time. The Calendar view shows which dates you added words, with visual indicators (colored diamonds) representing how many words you collected on each day. Click any date to see the words you added that day. This creates a visual history of your linguistic journey.

### 6. Tag Cloud & Full-Text Search

Organize words with flexible tags (e.g., "philosophy," "emotion," "nature"). The Tag Cloud view shows all your tags in a frequency-weighted visualization—larger tags mean more words tagged with that label.

Search across all fields—not just the word itself. Find words by their meaning, the context they appear in, their source, their tags, or their location. This enables serendipitous discovery and pattern recognition.

### 7. Queue for Exploration ("To Be Explored")

Sometimes you encounter a word but don't have time to fully explore it right then. The Queue (called "To Be Explored" in the app) is a holding area for these words. Add a word quickly with just the word itself, then return to it later to fill in meaning, ratings, and context. This removes friction from the collection process—capture the word now, understand it later.

### 8. Share Collections with Others

Select any words from your collection and create a shareable link. Others can view your curated words in a beautiful, interactive card view—without needing to log in. Share a collection of words about love, words from a specific author, or words you've learned in a language. Sharing sparks conversation about language and meaning.

### 9. Import & Export

Export your entire collection as JSON for backup or migration. Import collections from other sources. Your data remains yours.

## Design Philosophy

### Minimalism with Depth

The interface is clean and minimal—no unnecessary decoration, no visual clutter. But beneath the surface lies profound depth: multidimensional ratings, contextual fields, cross-language pairing, mode switching. The app respects your intelligence and invites exploration.

### Intentional Typography

The app uses serif fonts (Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond) for titles and quotes. Serif fonts evoke tradition, literature, and thoughtfulness. They signal that this is a space for contemplation, not quick consumption.

### Dark Theme with Teal Accents

The dark background reduces eye strain and creates an intimate, focused environment. The teal accent color suggests calm, clarity, and intellectual depth—colors associated with contemplation and understanding.

### Mobile-First & Responsive

The app works seamlessly on mobile, tablet, and desktop. Whether you're collecting words on your phone during a commute or reviewing your collection on a desktop, the experience is thoughtful and intentional.

## Part 2: For Developers — Technical Architecture

### Technology Stack

Name of the Words is built on a modern, full-stack JavaScript foundation:

| Layer | Technology | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Frontend** | React 19 + TypeScript | Component-based UI with type safety |
| **Styling** | Tailwind CSS 4 | Utility-first CSS framework for responsive design |
| **API Protocol** | tRPC 11 | End-to-end type-safe RPC framework eliminating REST boilerplate |
| **Backend** | Express 4 + Node.js | Lightweight HTTP server for API and OAuth handling |
| **Database** | MySQL (TiDB compatible) | Relational database for persistent storage |
| **ORM** | Drizzle ORM | Type-safe SQL query builder with zero-runtime overhead |
| **Authentication** | Manus OAuth | Built-in OAuth provider for user authentication |
| **Testing** | Vitest | Fast unit test framework with TypeScript support |
| **Build Tool** | Vite | Fast development server and production bundler |

### Database Design

The database schema reflects the app's core concepts:

#### Users Table
Stores user identity and authentication state, keyed by Manus OAuth `openId` (unique identifier from OAuth provider). Each user has a role (user or admin) for future permission-based features.

#### Words Table
The central table storing word entries. Key columns:

- **word:** The word itself (e.g., "serendipity")
- **originLanguage:** Language of the word (e.g., "english", "mandarin", "cantonese")
- **meaning, context, source, location:** Rich contextual metadata
- **ratingEssence, ratingBeauty, ratingSubtlety:** Three-dimensional ratings (0–12 each)
- **tags:** JSON array of user-defined tags (stored as text, parsed in application layer)
- **pairedWord, pairedMeaning:** Cross-language pairing fields
- **userId:** Foreign key to users table for data isolation
- **color:** Auto-generated hex color for visual identity in calendar and source views
- **dateAdded:** ISO date string (YYYY-MM-DD) for calendar grouping
- **isWork:** Boolean flag (0 or 1) indicating Work Mode vs Normal Mode
- **sourceMode:** Enum ("normal", "work", "mutual-arising") for flexible mode assignment
- **isQueued:** Boolean flag (0 or 1) indicating whether word is in the Queue ("To Be Explored")

#### Tags Table
Lookup table for tag autocomplete and tag cloud generation. Stores unique tag names.

#### User Preferences Table
Stores per-user settings, currently tracking `workMode` (boolean) to persist the user's mode preference across sessions.

#### Shared Decks Table
Stores metadata for shared word collections. Each deck has a unique token (URL-safe string), optional title, JSON-encoded array of word IDs, owner user ID, and creation timestamp. This table enables public sharing without requiring authentication.

### Architecture Rationale

**Why tRPC?** tRPC eliminates the gap between frontend and backend. Procedures are defined once on the server, then consumed directly on the client with full TypeScript type inference. No manual API contract files, no Axios wrappers, no type duplication. Changes to a procedure signature are immediately caught by TypeScript.

**Why MySQL + Drizzle?** MySQL is reliable, scalable, and well-understood. Drizzle ORM provides type-safe query building without the overhead of ORMs like Sequelize or TypeORM. Schema is version-controlled in code, and migrations are tracked in the `drizzle/migrations/` directory.

**Why JSON for tags?** Tags are stored as a JSON array within the words table rather than a separate junction table. This simplifies queries (no joins needed for basic word retrieval) while maintaining flexibility. Tag autocomplete and tag cloud generation query the JSON field directly.

**Why per-word colors?** Each word receives a unique auto-generated hex color on creation. This color is used in the Calendar view (diamond indicators) and Source view (source chips) to provide visual continuity and identity. Colors are stored in the database to ensure consistency across sessions.

**Why dual mode columns (isWork + sourceMode)?** The `isWork` boolean provides a simple flag for filtering. The `sourceMode` enum ("normal", "work", "mutual-arising") provides granular control for future features. Currently, "mutual-arising" words appear in both Normal and Work mode views.

### API Architecture

All API traffic flows through `/api/trpc`, making it easy to route at the edge or add middleware. Procedures are organized by feature:

- **auth:** Login/logout, user info
- **words:** CRUD operations, search, calendar data, random word
- **tags:** Tag list, tag cloud, tag-based search
- **preferences:** Get/set user settings (e.g., work mode preference)
- **share:** Create/retrieve/delete shared decks
- **stats:** Public statistics (Seekers, Words Named)

All procedures that access user data are wrapped in `protectedProcedure`, which injects the current user from the OAuth session. Word queries are automatically scoped to the current user's `userId` to ensure data isolation.

### Authentication Flow

1. User clicks "Login" on the landing page
2. Frontend redirects to `/api/oauth/callback` with Manus OAuth state
3. Backend validates OAuth response and creates or updates user in the database
4. Session cookie is set (signed with `JWT_SECRET`)
5. Subsequent requests include the session cookie, which is validated and decoded to extract the current user
6. All word operations are scoped to the current user's ID

### Data Isolation & Security

- **User-scoped queries:** All word queries filter by `userId` to prevent cross-user data access
- **Ownership checks:** Update and delete operations verify that the requesting user owns the word before proceeding
- **Public endpoints:** Stats and shared deck endpoints are public (no auth required)
- **Protected procedures:** Word CRUD, preferences, and user info require authentication

### Deployment Considerations

The app is deployed as a serverless application on Manus infrastructure. Key considerations:

- **Stateless:** No in-memory state; all data persists to MySQL
- **Environment variables:** Database connection, OAuth credentials, and API keys are injected as environment variables
- **Cold starts:** Vite dev server and Express server start quickly; no heavy initialization
- **Database connection pooling:** MySQL connections are pooled to handle concurrent requests efficiently

### Development Workflow

1. **Schema-first:** Update `drizzle/schema.ts` with new tables or columns
2. **Generate migration:** Run `pnpm db:push` to generate and apply migrations
3. **Add storage helpers:** Implement query helpers in `server/storage.ts`
4. **Add procedures:** Define tRPC procedures in `server/routers.ts`
5. **Wire UI:** Build React components in `client/src/pages/` and call procedures via `trpc.*.useQuery/useMutation`
6. **Test:** Write vitest tests in `server/*.test.ts` to verify business logic
7. **Deploy:** Push to main branch; Manus CI/CD handles deployment

### Key Design Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **JSON tags instead of junction table** | Simpler queries, faster reads, sufficient for current scale |
| **Auto-generated colors per word** | Provides visual identity and consistency without user configuration |
| **isQueued flag for exploration queue** | Allows words to be marked as "not yet fully explored" without deleting them |
| **sourceMode enum for flexible modes** | Enables future expansion (e.g., project-specific modes) without schema changes |
| **Manus OAuth for authentication** | Built-in provider eliminates need for external OAuth setup or password management |
| **tRPC for API** | Type-safe, reduces boilerplate, catches errors at compile time |
| **Drizzle ORM** | Lightweight, type-safe, version-controlled migrations, no runtime overhead |
